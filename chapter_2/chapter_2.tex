\section{Research Methodlogies: Theory and Computation}
I have already finished reading through the relevant lecture notes provided by my supervisor. These notes cover the key concepts and techniques in formal languages and automata theory.

The next step is to delve deeper into the topic of varieties of formal languages. I am currently studying a book titled ``Varieties of Formal Languages" by Jean-Éric Pin. This book provides a comprehensive overview of the theory of varieties and their applications in formal languages.
This text will be the main reference for understanding how regular languages can be classified using algebraic structures.

In addition to this, I plan to consult the following textbooks/research methods:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Samuel Eilenberg's ``Automata, Languages, and Machines" \cite{EilenbergSamuel1974Alam} which provides foundational knowledge on automata theory and formal languages.
    \item John M. Howie's ``Automata and Languages" \cite{alma9943532370001381} which is a gentle introduction to the subject.
    \item I will use the lecture notes of Jean-Éric Pin called ``Mathematical Foundations of Automata Theory" \cite{Pin2025} available online. This book contains Reiterman's theorem and other results relevant to the membership problem
    \item I will also look into research papers into the membership problem for pseudovarieties of finite monoids to understand the current state of research in this area.
\end{itemize}

Using literature, I will do 2 things:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item {\bf Theoretical study:} I will study the structure of syntactic monoids of regular languages and explore how varieties and pseudovarieties are defined and classified. In particular, I will examine Eilenberg’s correspondence in detail to understand how it provides a framework for classifying regular languages through the properties of their syntactic monoids.
    \item {\bf Computational study:} I will use computational tools such as GAP \cite{GAP4} to experiment and compute syntactic monoids of regular languages of small DFAs. I will try to implement algorithms to test membership in certain pseudovarieties of finite monoids and analyze their efficiency and effectiveness. Through this I want to shed light on the membership problem and see if it will yield new examples/insights.
\end{enumerate}
